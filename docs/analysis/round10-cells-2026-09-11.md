## What this run settled

1. **R10-A** — Claude predicted the BUY−SELL matched-basket contrast at **−0.74%, SE 0.31, t −2.4**
   from our published summary rows alone. Measured: **−0.7413%, SE 0.2914, t −2.54, p 0.011**;
   clean windows **−0.8556, t −2.59**. First contrast in ten rounds with |t| > 2 on both samples.
2. **R10-B** — the Wald check. On the full book `mean(bench) = drift × mean(T)` to **ratio 1.00**,
   so the paired benchmark carries no exit-selection bias; the deviation is entirely **directional**,
   which an exit artifact cannot produce. ⇒ §12.31c's α is a genuine measurement.
3. **R10-C** — decomposition: ⭐ **88% of the BUY book's gross loss is TAPE, not ALPHA**
   (raw −0.1877% = tape −0.1659% + alpha −0.0218%). The SELL book is the reverse.
4. **R10-D** — ⛔ the counter-timing is **not conditionable**: BUY vs SELL **trailing** tape at entry
   gives t = **+0.50 / −0.88 / +0.29** at 5/10/20 sessions. No observable antecedent ⇒ not
   actionable. **This also refutes the market-regime overlay's hypothesis on this sample** (best
   BUY cell t = +1.66, no cell clears 2) — and shows it was never gated on `index_ohlcv_1d`.
5. **R10-E** — the one bridging mechanism (basket short-horizon mean reversion, `t = −2.14`)
   predicts **−0.022%**, or **3%** of the −0.741% observed.

⇒ ⭐ **RECORDED, NOT ACTED ON.** At t = −2.06 clustered against this programme's own t ≈ 3.6 bar,
a forward-tape difference with no observable antecedent is treated as an artifact until it clears
— the same verdict RVOL got, for the same reason.
