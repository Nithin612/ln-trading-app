# M93 audit — the six-review round, validated against the data

**2026-09-21. READ-ONLY.** Six external reviews (ChatGPT · Gemini · DeepSeek · Grok · Claude ·
Kimi) converged on one instruction: *stop asking models, get the five facts, freeze one page, run
once.* They were right that facts were the bottleneck. **They underestimated how decisive the
facts would be.**

⭐ Every review is treated here by **validating its claims against our repo and data**, not by
ranking opinions. Four queries settle the candidate.

---

## 1. ⭐⭐ FACT ZERO — M93's return window is the TRADEABLE one

Every reviewer put this first, and several guessed wrong. Read from
`scripts/signed_displacement_study.py`:

```sql
lead(o.open, 1)  OVER w AS fill         -- tomorrow's OPEN
lead(o.close, 1) OVER w AS exit_close   -- tomorrow's CLOSE  (H=1)
gap_pct = (fill - close)/close          -- last night's gap
fwd_pct = (exit_close - fill)/fill      -- open -> close, SAME session
```

⇒ **h=1 is open-to-close on the gap day — reading A, the one the proposed trade touches.**
⛔ Kimi's inference that *"is followed by … next day"* leans to the untradeable close-to-close
reading is **refuted by the code**. The question was the right one to ask; the guess was wrong.

## 2. ⛔⛔ FACT ONE — the standard error is iid, and clustering deflates t by up to 8.6×

`stddev(fwd_pct)/sqrt(count(*))` — **stock-days treated as independent.** Gap days cluster by
session almost by construction: an index-wide move produces hundreds of same-direction gaps on one
morning (measured: **max 243 events in a single session**).

Recomputed with `block_bootstrap.cluster_robust_mean_t` (our own estimator, validated against a
known null):

| arm | events | sessions | mean | t IID *(M93's method)* | **t CLUSTERED** | deflation |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| gap ≤ −2% (long) | 10,049 | 1,211 | +0.6505% | +14.33 | **+1.66** | **8.6×** |
| gap ≥ +2% (short) | 12,053 | 1,460 | +0.6145% | +17.69 | +4.99 | 3.5× |
| pooled sign-adjusted | 22,102 | 1,610 | +0.6309% | +22.52 | **+3.33** | 6.8× |

⭐ The reconstruction reproduces M93 exactly (+0.6505% / t +14.33 against the published +0.650% /
+14.3), so this is the same measurement with the SE corrected.

⭐⭐ **Kimi predicted "t ≈ 14 → roughly 1–3" and was right.** The single most valuable point made
across all six reviews. **The long arm — the one recommended as "the strongest existing evidence"
— is not significant.** Pooled sits at 3.33, below this project's own t ≈ 3.6 bar.

## 3. ⚠ FACT TWO — trade-weighting vs session-weighting moves t from 3.33 to 8.65

ChatGPT alone flagged this as *"a real design choice, not something to silently assume."* It is
worth more than a design detail:

| session-level estimand (n = 1,610) | mean | σ_session | t |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| all events in the session | +0.4631% | 2.149% | +8.65 |
| top 3 by \|gap\| | +0.7284% | 2.873% | +10.17 |
| random 3 | +0.4674% | 2.551% | +7.36 |
| event-weighted, cluster-robust | +0.6309% | — | **+3.33** |

⭐ **The structural argument settles it independently of the numbers**: a ₹1 lakh book with a
3-position cap cannot take 243 positions. Capital is deployed **per session**, so a session is one
observation ⇒ session-weighted. ⚠ **But I saw all four numbers before making that argument**, and
that is recorded rather than hidden. Choosing *top-3-by-|gap|* over *random-3* would be
optimisation and is refused.

## 4. ⛔⛔ FACT THREE — on the cohort and period we can actually trade, the effect is ~2.7× smaller

M93 runs on the annual top-250 of `ohlcv_1d` across 2020–2026. The tradeable cohort is the ~210
names with 5-minute capture, and only from 2023-07-03.

| | full M93 sample | **tradeable cohort, modern block** |
|---|--:|--:|
| events / sessions | 22,102 / 1,610 | 7,436 / **753** |
| name retention | — | **94.4%** (the cohort is NOT the problem) |
| session-level mean | +0.4631% | **+0.1743%** |
| σ_session | 2.149% | 2.319% |
| **t** | +8.65 | **+2.06** |

⭐ The headline comes substantially from **2020–2022**, which our own records already flag as a
different regime (COVID +38.1%/yr · 2021 +34.7% · 2022 −0.1%/yr).

## 5. ⛔⛔ THE ARITHMETIC THAT ENDS IT — required gross is 2.6× what is measured

Power, computed from our own numbers as every reviewer demanded (**no invented 450/470**):

```
required net μ for t = 3.6 on n = 753 at σ = 2.319%
    = 3.6 × 2.319 / √753            = 0.304 % per session
plus measured cost, ~14 bps/position = 0.140 %
⇒ required GROSS                     = 0.444 % per session
measured GROSS (tradeable cohort)    = 0.174 % per session
```

⭐⭐ **The executable strategy would have to deliver 2.6× more than the full daily window
actually contains.** And the executable window is a strict *subset* of that daily window — entry
at 09:20 forgoes the first five minutes (where gap reversion is most concentrated) and exit at
15:00 forgoes the last thirty.

⇒ **Net of measured cost the candidate is ~+0.03%/session, t ≈ 0.4. It is refuted before a single
line of strategy code was written.**

⚠ **Not a strict upper bound** — a truncated window could in principle contain more than the whole.
It is not plausible, and saying so is a judgement, not a measurement. The one measurement that
would settle it (09:20 → 15:00 on `ohlcv_5m`) is cheap, and is the only reason to keep the file open.

## 7. ⛔ Kimi's arithmetic audit — the method was right, three of four findings are wrong

Kimi recomputed everything, which is the correct posture. But it assumed a ₹20,000 notional when
the computation used **₹19,780 — 20 shares × ₹989, because you cannot buy a fraction of a share**:

| Kimi's finding | verdict |
|---|---|
| "₹20.97 / 10.60 bps mismatch; 20.97/20,000 = 10.49" | ⛔ **wrong** — 20.97/19,780 = 10.60 ✓ |
| "₹11.87 vs ₹12.00 brokerage, ₹0.13 unexplained" | ⛔ **wrong** — 0.03% × 19,780 × 2 = ₹11.868 ✓ |
| "~185 names vs 6,748/37 = 182.4" | ⛔ **wrong** — 185 is the **median** cohort per window, not a mean |
| "22.22 bps STT not derivable" | ⚠ fair — it is a project figure (delivery statutory floor), not derived in that message |

⇒ **"Three small numerical loosenesses is a pattern" collapses.** ⭐ **The lesson generalises: an
auditor without the inputs manufactures discrepancies.** Publish the notional, not just the bps.

⭐ Kimi's other new point — that the [1.58, 1.93] interval's dependence structure is part of the
estimand — was already handled: the bootstrap clusters whole windows, and quant-verifier confirmed
that is the **widest** of the three options (window [1.58, 1.93] · name [1.68, 1.82] · iid
[1.71, 1.78]).

## ⛔ Bottom line

**Overnight-gap reversion is refuted as the successor candidate**, on M93's own data, under its own
most favourable framing, before any strategy code existed. The +0.650% headline does not survive
**any** of the three corrections independently — session clustering (t 1.66), restriction to the
tradeable cohort and modern period (+0.174%, t 2.06), or measured costs (net ~+0.03%, t ≈ 0.4).

⭐ **The reviews were right that the facts were the bottleneck. Four queries were worth more than
six rounds of design.** The pre-registration machinery they built is sound and should be kept — it
is what the *next* candidate gets, and it now has real σ and n inputs to work with.

⚠ **What this does NOT say:** nothing here tests 09:20 → 15:00 on intraday bars, and nothing here
speaks to other intraday shapes. This is one candidate, refuted.
