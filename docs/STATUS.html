Part I
The system, end to end

What the platform is and how every layer fits together — architecture, the signal engine, the confluence rule that governs what may be traded, the eight eligibility gates, the Rust core, realtime, and the constraints that bind them. This part describes how it works; it changes only when the system does.

01 · What this is

What this is, in one paragraph #

A personal intelligent stock-suggestion and algo-trading platform for the Indian markets (NSE/BSE). It ingests market data — end-of-day and live tick-by-tick — runs every stock through a weighted confluence signal engine that only fires when several independent technical factors agree at the same price level, sizes each idea off a mandatory stop-loss rule, filters it through eight downstream eligibility gates, and surfaces the result as an actionable suggestion: entry, stop, target, quantity, and why.

Trades execute in paper mode today. Live order placement is gated behind an explicit opt-in plus a proven profitable paper record. Built solo, for personal use first, with the option to productise later.

Guiding principle

Profits cannot be promised, but losses are controlled by construction — mandatory sizing, max-stop rejection, class-correct validity, eight eligibility gates, and a non-disableable circuit breaker. Every one of those is enforced in code, not by discipline.

Process model

Four long-lived processes plus two datastores: FastAPI (REST + WebSocket fan-out) · live-worker (KiteTicker plus the embedded Rust LiveEngine; restarts daily at token expiry with warm-up and gap-fill) · Celery worker + beat (EOD ingestion, nightly signals, expiry sweeps, the pair mint, the CAS capture) · Postgres and Redis.

The tick hot path is deliberately boring concurrency: ticker thread → queue.Queue → consumer thread → one PyO3 call per tick batch → sync Redis pipeline. No asyncio in the hot path; two of v1's four live bugs were asyncio/thread impedance mismatches. One trap worth naming: live_worker.py is the production path and tick_consumer.py is the dormant v1 one — a new live feature wired only into the latter ships inert, which nearly happened to the depth capture in 6.8.1.

05 · Signal engine

The signal engine — our edge #

The signal-engine specification document is the heart of the system: hook-protected in the repo, changed only by explicit instruction plus a backtest regression.

The philosophy: confluence, not single indicators

A signal is multiple independent indicators agreeing at the same price level. "RSI below 30, buy" is noise — it is why most retail traders lose. The edge is requiring several conditions to align. Every signal carries four parts: direction, classification, levels (entry, stop, target, quantity), and justification (which factors fired, their scores, the confidence percentage).

The 14-factor universe

Each factor returns a score from −1.0 (strongly bearish) to +1.0 (strongly bullish). 0.0 means "not applicable" and is excluded from the weight denominator — a distinction that turns out to matter enormously (§6).

The confluence scorer

# weighted average of ONLY the factors that actually scored
total_weighted = sum(f.weight * f.score for f in factors)
total_weight   = sum(f.weight for f in factors if f.score != 0)   # ← the denominator
normalized     = total_weighted / total_weight        # −1 .. +1
confidence_pct = int(abs(normalized) * 100)           # int() truncation is canon
if confidence_pct < 70: return None            # the gate
direction = "BUY" if normalized > 0 else "SELL"

The scorer takes a weighted average of only those factors that had something to say, then requires the result to clear 70%. The consequence of "only the factors that had something to say" is the subject of the next section.

Weight semantics

Every factor carries its own weight independently, and the denominator is the sum of weights of the factors that actually scored. So four factors firing decisively beats fourteen firing weakly. Sharing group weights was measured to loosen the gate badly (roughly 400 extra weak trades) and was rejected.

ADX regime adjustments: ADX below 20 (weak trend) requires 5 percentage points of extra confidence; ADX above 40 (strong trend) drops the threshold to 65%. ATR(14) above 3% of price reduces position size by 25% — a risk rule, not an alpha rule.

Classification, validity, sizing

  • Classification by strongest-contributing timeframe: 1m/5m → scalp · 15m/1h → intraday · 1d → swing · 1d with Multibagger EMA or 1w → positional.
  • Validity: scalp 30 minutes · intraday until 3:15 PM IST · swing 5 trading days · positional 30 trading days. Trading-day arithmetic uses the NSE holiday calendar — calendar-day maths is a bug. An expiry sweeper runs every five minutes.
  • Sizing (mandatory): qty = floor(capital × risk% / |entry − SL|), where risk_pct is a whole percent (2.0 means 2%). This is why a wider stop is not "more risk" — it automatically buys a smaller position, and the rupees at stake are unchanged. That property does real work in §18.
  • Reject, never clamp: if the natural stop exceeds the class cap (scalp/intraday 0.5%, swing 8%), the signal is rejected. The stop is never tightened to fit.
  • Trailing: at the first target, close half and move the stop to breakeven; at the next, book a quarter more and trail again. Per user, an absolute-rupee profit-lock ladder can replace the fixed trail.
06 · The multi-factor rule

The rule we adopted last week: never one factor alone #

This is the most important behavioural decision of the last fortnight, and it is worth explaining slowly because the reason is counter-intuitive.

The stated rule was always "confluence only — never a single indicator". It was written into the spec and into the project's hard constraints. It was not being enforced. The 70% confidence gate, which everyone assumed enforced it, cannot.

Why a 70% gate does not mean "several factors agreed"

Look again at the denominator in §5: total_weight sums only the weights of factors whose score is non-zero. That is deliberate and correct — a Fibonacci factor should not dilute the score just because there was no retracement to measure. But it has a consequence:

07 · The gates

The eight eligibility gates — where behaviour is decided #

This is the section to read if you want to know what the system will and will not do. The signal engine proposes; these eight gates dispose. Each one is a small, pure module downstream of the frozen engine, and each has a three-position switch:

the three modes

 off — a true no-op. No read, no compute, no footprint at all.

 shadow — evaluates and stamps its verdict on the order, but never blocks. This is how every gate earns its place.

 active — rejects the order with a 409. Behaviour-changing, so it needs sign-off, and it is reversible by one env var plus a restart.

the two invariants every gate obeys

Fail open. A gate that cannot form a view — no price, no history, a database fault — lets the trade through. Every database read runs inside a begin_nested() savepoint so a failure rolls back only that read and leaves the session usable. A missing benchmark must never suppress a trade.

Leave an audit trail. In shadow or active, the verdict is written to orders.broker_payload, so a nightly sidecar can aggregate exactly what each gate would have done and any past decision can be reconstructed.

10 · Paper, risk & fills

Paper trading, risk, and how honest the fills are #

Paper mode is the default and the only path today. place_order is paper-only; there is no Kite order or GTT path. That is Phase 7 and it has not started. The machinery that controls losses by construction:

  • Mandatory position sizing off the stop distance, risk-first from the actual fill, so a chased fill shrinks the quantity instead of over-risking the account. Repeat entries cannot stack past the budget.
  • Max-stop rejection — signals whose natural stop exceeds the class cap are rejected, not clamped.
  • Honest fills — a gap-through exit books the gapped market price, not the stop; the fill candle is checked intrabar; the stop is checked before the target.
  • The daily-loss circuit breaker — never disableable, never weakened, never configurable-off. Not for tests, not on request.
  • Exit governance, per user — either a fixed trail-stop ladder or an absolute-rupee profit-lock ladder (breakeven at +₹2,000, seal peak −₹1,000 once peak ≥ ₹3,000, ATR room ×2.0).
  • Corporate actions on open positions — a split or bonus adjusts a held paper position on its ex-date while preserving R exactly: entry and peak divide by the factor, and the stop and target move by distance × old_qty / new_qty so R stays exact even when a fractional entitlement floors the quantity. Ratios are admin-verified only; the ex-date worker is idempotent and catches up.

The fill model got materially more honest — and it reset the clock

Until 2026-08-17 a paper fill was the reference price plus a flat 2 basis points. Since 6.8.2 the fill is priced off the real order book: the actual half-spread plus a size-versus-top-of-book impact term, with the flat 2 bps kept as a floor so a fill is never cheaper than under the old model, failing open to flat when no fresh depth is cached.

why it mattered

82.1% of 1,666 live NSE books had a half-spread wider than the flat 2 bps. Median spread 11.85 bps; p90 87; p99 280. The flat model was not a simplification — it was a systematic overstatement of the edge.

the consequence

Paper P&L before and after 2026-08-17 is not comparable. The book was flattened at the 15:29 close that day (26 positions, net +₹7,222.66) and the clock reset. Backtests are untouched — they run on candle data and never read depth.

Section 9 of every daily report now prints, per fill, the half-spread, the impact term, the total in basis points, and the rupees the flat model had been under-charging. On 2026-08-25 that was ₹687 across 7 fills, all 7 priced off a live book.

The go-live gate

Live trading requires explicit opt-in and a 30-day profitable paper record, enforced in code. The clock has not started. Closing Phase 6.8 and merging to main did not start it — day 1 is deferred until the user explicitly says "proceed". Phase 7 will also replace "30 calendar days" with something better: N trades across at least two regimes, positive expectancy, bounded drawdown.

Honesty note

No system guarantees profits. The truthful adjudicated baseline on 2023–26 daily data is negative — the old flattering result was fill-flattery. Profitability is the job of the strategy profiles (the rrbo swing profile walk-forwards positive at +41.3% / 1.97 Sharpe) and of entry-selection tuning, measured against reality rather than asserted.

Rules that have each cost real time when broken

  • flush() makes rows visible to this session; commit() makes them real. Side effects that depend on visibility — Celery dispatch, Redis publish — fire after the commit.

15 · Hard constraints

Hard constraints — the non-negotiables #

These override convenience, deadlines and "just this once". They are enforced by code and by commit hooks, not by memory.

  1. The signal spec is the edge and is hook-protected. Changes require explicit instruction plus a backtest regression.
  2. Confluence only. No signal from a single indicator, ever. Everything flows through the weighted scorer at a ≥70% gate. As of 2026-08-18 this is finally enforced rather than merely stated (§6).
  3. No look-ahead. Compute on candle N, valid from N+1; backtests fill at the N+1 open; committed signals only from complete candles. The tick-level provisional layer is labelled as such end to end and never enters a backtest or P&L.
  4. Position sizing on every signal, with risk_pct as a whole percent. Reject, never clamp a stop that exceeds the class cap.
  5. Money is Decimal / Numeric(12,4) / i64×1e-4. Storage UTC, market logic IST, trading-day arithmetic via the NSE calendar.
  6. Paper is the default. Live requires opt-in plus 30 profitable paper days. The daily-loss circuit breaker is never disableable.
  7. Every feature ships with tests and follows the vertical slice: model → migration → service → API → frontend → tests.
The workbench enforces this

Commit hooks auto-format on edit, guard destructive commands, and refuse edits to protected files — the signal spec, applied migrations, .env. Five review agents cover the risk surfaces: a quant verifier for anything touching analysis, signals, backtest or the engine; a bug hunter for pipeline, async and broker changes; a UI reviewer; a performance auditor for hot paths; and a test guardian for coverage honesty. If a hook blocks an edit, it is working — the correct response is to route around it, not to disable it. It blocked one edit while this page was being written, for mentioning a protected filename in a shell command.

Being hit meant something. The setup was simply wrong, and no stop placement rescues that.

The mechanism, in one sentence

A stop narrower than one average session's range is not a stop — it is a coin flip with a fee. Ordinary daily noise reaches it, so the exit says nothing about whether the setup was right; when the stop sits outside the noise band, being hit is genuine information.

Part 4 — so should we widen the stops? No.

This is where the analysis nearly went wrong, and the error is worth showing because it is a trap anyone would fall into.

method · the mistake and the fixWhy the first counterfactual was meaningless
  1. First attempt: replay every stop-out loser with a wider stop, holding quantity constant. Result: total loss goes from −₹59,834 to −₹122,500. Conclusion drawn: widening stops is catastrophic.
  2. That conclusion was an artifact. The platform sizes risk-first — qty = capital × risk% / |entry − SL| (§5). A wider stop automatically buys a smaller position. Holding quantity constant while widening the stop does not test stop placement; it tests taking a bigger bet.
  3. The fix: measure in R. R is price distance divided by stop distance, so it is immune to the sizing confound and it is the platform's native unit. Rupee risk stays at ₹2,000 in every variant, exactly as the live sizer would have it.

Redone properly, over the whole 82-trade cohort — winners included, because widening a stop shrinks a winner's R just as it rescues a loser's:

The satisfying part — this reproduces a gate we already built

Two independent measurements agreeing is the strongest argument this gate has. It is still not enough to flip it: readiness requires 20 resolved flagged trades and stands at 12, and a flip needs explicit sign-off. Recommendation: keep accruing, and treat 1.0× as the supported threshold when the flip is considered.

Part 5 — the other half: we grade a 20-day trade on a one-day clock

The desk's instinct was that these names were "behaving like swing and positional trades". The tape agrees. The trades need days; the daily report scores them in hours.

This changes how the daily report should be read

"0/5 reached ≥1R" has led every daily report for three weeks, and it is an entry-day statistic being read as a verdict on the strategy. Twelve percent on the entry day and fifty-four percent over a positional horizon are the same trades on two different clocks. A positional trade on day 5 of 20 is unresolved, not failed — and GRSE (day 5 of 20, −₹536) and HEROMOTOCO (day 5 of 20, −₹672) in §17 are exactly that.

But the race is close, and that is the real problem. Within each trade's own horizon, which arrives first — the +1R or the stop?

  • n is small: 82 closed trades, 24 positional, 17 stop-out losers. Every group number has wide error bars.

  • Daily bars only. No intraday ordering; stop-before-target assumed throughout.

Three findings that came out of these loops

trade fresh — this one is actionable

How far into a signal's validity window we entered, against outcome: the 20–40% elapsed band returned +₹7,182 at a 67% win rate, and every band past 40% is net-negative. Monotonic. This finding is already folded into the Opportunities table's conviction score as an age-decay term.

the "losing streak" was not degradation

A two-window autopsy (04–12 vs 13–21 August) found the recent run was n=4 plus a down-drifting tape — NIFTY had one up day in eight — not degraded selection. §18 now supplies the mechanism for those four.

a hypothesis we killed

A 3-year regime study over 575 sessions found below-200-DMA plus weak breadth had the best forward 20-day return (+1.33% vs +0.21%) — textbook mean reversion, and the opposite of the two-window hypothesis. Then a block bootstrap killed it: P(weak > strong) = 83%, short of significance, resting on about two overlapping episodes, and the non-overlapping subsample flips sign. Two shadow layers are inert, and it is worth knowing why

The sector-RS gate needs about 21 sessions of index history and the market-regime gate needs about 200 for its 200-day average. The catch-up ingestion only reaches back 21 days, so both are running on insufficient history until a deep backfill is run — scripts/backfill_indices.py 2023-07-01 <today>, once. Their verdicts in the meantime (including the two "blocked" stamps on BHARTIARTL in §7) are computed from a short window and should not be weighted heavily.

A decision made against the obvious answer

The liquidity gate — built, measured, and deliberately not switched on

The liquidity gate was built to stop the SRTL archetype: a name too illiquid to exit. A deep dive on 2026-08-21 then found the opposite of what was expected. The illiquid set was net-positive and the liquid set net-negative, robust across floors from ₹50 lakh to ₹5 crore, on median and on win rate — and the worst losses were in liquid names. Moreover SRTL is the only trade that was both illiquid and diversity-flagged, so the already-active diversity gate catches that archetype without it.

Decision: keep it in shadow and reframe it later as a position-sizing and slippage modifier rather than an entry filter. Realised P&L over a short benign sample cannot measure what liquidity actually protects against — tail risk and exitability — so pricing it as an entry gate is the wrong instrument. This finding also casts doubt on the planned market-cap size floor: cross-tab a cheap proxy before paying for an XBRL scraper.

23 · CAS

The Closing Auction Session — built, now watching #

This is the active piece of work, and it is the reason the project is in watch mode this week.

What changed in the market

NSE introduced a Closing Auction Session on 3 August 2026. The facts, verified against the circulars rather than the popular write-ups (which contradict each other on timing):

  • A 20-minute call auction, 3:15 PM → 3:35 PM. Continuous trading in the covered stocks now ends at 3:15, not 3:30.
  • Category-I stocks only — those with active F&O contracts. Everything else still closes the old way.
  • Reference price is the VWAP of trades between 3:00 and 3:15, falling back to last price then prior close.
  • The auction discovers a single equilibrium closing price — the price that maximises matchable volume — replacing the old weighted average of the last thirty minutes. The window has a random end to deter gaming.
  • Equity derivatives now close at 3:40 PM. Cash and F&O now close on different mechanics and different clocks.
The implication for our data model

The 3:15 price is the last continuous-market price, and the official close is an auction print that can differ materially from it. Two distinct, both-meaningful numbers where there used to be one — so any EOD logic that assumed "the last tick is the close" is now subtly wrong for Category-I names.

What edge is actually available to us — and what is not

closed to us

Predicting the clearing price from live auction imbalance. The literature confirms imbalance predicts the clearing price — but only with a live imbalance feed during the auction. That is a colocation, low-latency, institutional game, and every other algo is running the same supply-and-demand reconstruction. After costs, "buy quantity exceeds sell quantity, therefore buy" is a loser. Retail does not win this race.

open to us

The overnight reversal of the auction move. Multiple studies find closing-auction returns are systematically reversed overnight — around 14% of the auction return gives back, persisting more than two hours into the next session. Part of the auction move is transient order pressure, not information. A stock pushed up into the close tends to open softer; one pushed down tends to bounce. That is exactly our timeframe — buy near the close, sell the next day.

  • Stage 0Probe — CONFIRMED 2026-08-25. Does the data even exist through our broker? Yes. Timing measured live: fields populate around 15:21, execution around 15:29. Gotcha found: ohlc.close is the prior day's close; the true close is last-price or indicative after 15:29. done
  • Stage 1Capture — BUILT 2026-08-25. A cas_daily table and a market-hours Celery task that self-guards to 15:15–15:33 IST over the F&O universe, upserting per stock per day: it freezes the 3:15 price and keeps the last non-zero imbalance. 8 tests. The bug-hunter caught a HIGH here — the naive "latest imbalance" stored the post-match zero, which would have destroyed the predictor entirely. done
  • Stage 2Study — BLOCKED ON DATA. This is the watch. Once cas_daily has accrued, measure the next-day return from ohlcv_1d against the CAS move.   • Stage 4Participate in the auction — far off, gated on live trading existing at all.

CAS is never an intraday predictor. Not now, not after a successful study. 24 · MCE

The Market Context Engine — in progress #

Everything the engine currently knows is bottom-up: this stock's price, its volume, its patterns. It knows nothing about whether the sector is falling, the index is in a downtrend, or the company reports earnings tomorrow. The Market Context Engine is the top-down complement.

The design rule that makes this safe

Context enters as gates and modifiers, never as additive factors. News and sentiment have close to zero directional alpha but high veto value: they are good at saying "not this one, not today" and bad at saying "buy this". Adding a news score into the confluence sum would corrupt a frozen, parity-pinned engine with a noisy signal. Adding it as a gate downstream cannot.

  • 1Sector / index relative strength — the RS overlay. built · shadow
  • 2Index price store — an index_ohlcv_1d table plus a benchmark provider. Source decision: real index OHLC from the NSE indices CSV the VIX service already downloads — no new Kite dependency. built
  • 3Sector-RS shadow sidecar — the measurement loop, then the flip from off to shadow. built · shadow
  • 4Market-regime gate — the 200-day-average trend filter, with VIX carried as informational only. built · shadow
  • 5aLiquidity junk gate — median daily traded value floor. built · shadow, and deliberately staying there (§21)

  • 5bMarket-cap floor — needs a market-cap writer, which does not exist; the planned route is an XBRL scraper. Now in question: §21's liquidity finding suggests a size floor may be no better than a liquidity floor, so cross-tab a cheap proxy before paying for the scraper.
  • 6News veto — the original motivation, and last because it is the hardest to source honestly.

25 · Phase 7

Phase 7 — live trading, in detail #

The last phase, and the one with the most careful build order. Today place_order is paper-only: there is no Kite order path, no GTT path, no reconciliation, and no kill switch. Every "live" recommendation anywhere in the docs is a Phase-7 constraint, not a description of something running.

The build order, and why it is this order

  1. The risk engine first — as a single gate. Before any order can leave the building, one component must own every limit: per-trade risk, daily loss, portfolio heat, position count, instrument eligibility. One gate, not checks scattered across the call path. Built and tested against paper before the broker adapter exists at all.
  2. An execution engine behind a broker adapter. The order path talks to an interface; the Kite implementation is one adapter behind it, with the paper broker as another. This is the NautilusTrader lesson: the shape of the runtime, not its code.
  3. Reconciliation. On startup and on reconnect, the local position book must be reconciled against the broker's truth. Every live system's worst bug is a divergence it did not notice.
  4. The kill switch — tested, not merely present. A tested kill switch is a gate item; an untested one is decoration.
  5. Then, and only then, one stock. A single-instrument live pilot at minimum size, behind the opt-in and the earned paper record.
The gate, restated because it is easy to soften

Live requires explicit opt-in and a 30-day profitable paper record, enforced in code rather than promised. The clock has not started — merging Phase 6.8 did not start it, and day 1 waits on the user saying "proceed". Phase 7 will also replace the calendar rule with a better one: N trades across at least two regimes, positive expectancy, bounded drawdown. A calendar month of a quiet tape proves nothing.

27 · Glossary

Glossary #

R
One unit of the trade's own risk — |entry − stop| × quantity. A stop-out is −1R by design. R is the only unit that compares trades of different sizes and stop widths honestly, which is why §18 is measured in it.
MFE / MAE
Maximum favourable / adverse excursion — the best and worst marks a trade ever reached, whether or not it was exited there. The gap between MFE and the realised result is the give-back leak.
Confluence
Several independent factors agreeing at the same price level. The opposite of a single-indicator trigger, and the platform's core premise.
Confidence %
The weighted average of the factors that scored, as a percentage. Not "what fraction of evidence agrees" — see §6, which is the whole reason the diversity gate exists.
Scoring factor
A factor whose score is non-zero, meaning it had something to say. Factors scoring 0.0 are "not applicable" and are excluded from the denominator.
ADX regime
Trend strength. Below 20 is ranging; 20–25 is transitional (net-negative, and now blocked); above 25 trending; above 40 strongly trending.
ATR
Average true range — the stock's typical per-period movement. A stop measured in ATRs is a stop measured against the stock's own noise (§18).
Chase / chase_r
How far past the signal's entry the actual fill happened, in R. Above 0.33R the reward is gone but the risk is not — risk and reward move in opposite directions on a chase.
Give-back
Peak mark minus end-of-day mark on a position that faded. The number-one leak in the current book (§17).
Portfolio heat
The sum of risk-at-fill across all open positions, as a percentage of capital — what would be lost if every stop hit at once. Not capped across days (§26).
Shadow
A layer that runs on the real schedule and is measured to outcome, but which the order path refuses to trade. How every gate earns its place.
Eligibility overlay / gate
A pure module downstream of the frozen engine that can veto an order without altering the engine. Three modes — off, shadow, active — and always fail-open (§7).
Committed vs forming
Committed signals come from complete candles and are backtestable; forming signals are tick-level, labelled provisional, and never enter a backtest or P&L.
Walk-forward
Fit on the past, test on the untouched future, roll forward. The only backtest result that counts as evidence here.
Block bootstrap
Resampling in contiguous blocks to preserve autocorrelation. It is what killed the breadth headline in §21 — the naive result rested on about two overlapping episodes.
Parity
The Python↔Rust equality contract: 1e-9 on the EMA family, 1e-6 on the Wilder family, and exact on factor scores, confidence integers and signal decisions.
CAS
Closing Auction Session — NSE's 3:15–3:35 PM call auction, live since 3 August 2026 (§23).
Circuit band
The exchange's daily price limit. A long pinned near the lower band cannot be exited at any price, which is what the circuit gate refuses.
T2T / -BE series
Trade-to-trade scrips, excluded from the live universe. The exclusion is self-healing through the daily sync rather than a hand-maintained blacklist. A missing plain symbol usually means a series move — check for the suffixed form first.

The doc-sync rule, because drift has cost real time

On 2026-08-08 a stale status block claimed 974 tests and a phase "in progress" weeks after the truth had moved, and it cost hours. A document that disagrees with reality is worse than no document.
